P56: Abstract
P57: Since COVID-19 has increased user-generated reviews in Hong Kong's e-commerce sector, the role of AI in processing reviews has emerged. However, there is a significant gap in developing AI instruments meant only for traditional Chinese texts. This leads to the need for creating a project called "Lightweight AI for Smart Review Analysis." The proposed project will take advantage of DrissionPage, mBERT, and MySQL to design this Smart Review Analysis AI that automatically classifies the sentiment of reviews as positive, negative, and neutral, extracts product attribute keywords, builds multidimensional topic models, and presents the results through an easy-to-use interface. The proposed system will provide cost-efficient solutions for small and medium-sized merchants that require massive processing of customer data and transparency for consumers in buying decisions based on feedback free from any bias. This project will have long-term implications for promoting competition based on sustainable development in e-commerce. The data collection, cleaning, and storing processes have been completed, and modeling and AI-based report generation are about to be done.
P59: Introduction
P60: 1.1 Overview
P61: The pandemic, combined with the growth of eCommerce in the post-pandemic era, has led to an increase in user-generated reviews in ALL industries. The reviews play an important role in helping consumers make decisions on purchasing products/services; however, dealing with a lot of user-generated reviews has presented merchants with a great deal of challenges related to managing the volume of reviews and using the reviews to obtain actionable insight. Therefore, it is now essential for merchants to have a review analysis system that is capable of continuously improving their service quality and customer satisfaction based on the insights obtained from reviews.
P63: Currently, there are few AI models developed specifically for traditional Chinese review analysis within the Hong Kong market. The Hong Kong market lacks artificial intelligence models for analyzing traditional Chinese reviews, for the following reasons:
P65: Hong Kong uses standard characters (not simplified) and blends unique colloquial terms (e.g., Cantonese loanwords in written form) besides using standard characters. Major differences exist between traditional and simplified Chinese at the level of phrasing and semantics. High-quality annotated datasets for local e-commerce reviews are very rare. This is, in fact, the main barrier to model training since most corpora available use either simplified Chinese or general traditional Chinese texts and not specifically industry focused such as e-commerce feedback.
P67: Small and medium-sized merchants dominate Hong Kong’s e-commerce ecosystem. Since SMEs do not have ample budgets for custom AI solutions, the commercial incentive for tech providers to develop specialized models is weakened. In simple terms, large-scale monetization cannot be achieved.
P69: Therefore, there is a need in Hong Kong for intelligent tools to enable the automatic processing of user-generated reviews through technology such as sentiment analysis and topic modeling. The ability to automatically process user-generated reviews will allow merchants and consumers to quickly and easily access key information necessary to make informed decisions regarding the optimization of products/services, and to remain competitive within the market.
P70: 1.2 Project Aim
P71: The goal of this project is to create an AI-enabled lightweight Review Analysis Tool that processes and analyzes user reviews for ecommerce automatically. This system will perform Sentiment Analysis to quickly identify user sentiments on the feedback they have posted (whether that feedback is positive, negative or neutral), as well as track other associated measures (such as number of likes).
P73: The Review Analysis Tool will also allow ecommerce businesses to extract certain keywords regarding product attribute(s) from the reviews (i.e., size, material, price) and will provide a multi-dimensional approach to the analysis of these product attributes through Topic Modelling.
P75: Ultimately, this review analysis tool will enable businesses to better analyze their customers' needs to enhance and improve their products and/or services. Companies will be better able to provide their customers with the tools necessary for making informed decisions when purchasing products.
P76: 1.3 Project Objectives
P77: 1. The mBERT (multilingual BERT) model will be employed for sentiment analysis; it will allow you to retrieve the opinions of consumers automatically from their reviews, and determine if their opinion is either negative, positive, or neutral.
P79: The mBERT is a language model from Google that came out in 2018. It’s basically BERT but designed to handle multiple languages at once. The idea behind it was to make it easier for computers to understand and work with different languages without needing a separate model for each one. So, when you use mBERT, you’re tapping into a tool that’s built to grasp meanings across languages and can also handle tasks in just one language pretty well.
P81: For multilingual reviews related to the Hong Kong region (such as Cantonese, English, and Mandarin), mBERT can perform cross-language sentiment classification and topic modeling directly without additional adaptation. The model's automated text analysis capabilities can be combined with a visualization interface to quickly produce structured insights from the reviews.
P84: 2. Using models like mBERT, RoBERTa, DistilmBERT, topic modeling will provide the ability to analyze the opinions of consumers and identify topics that are present within their reviews. It will classify the opinions of consumers based on their sentiment (positive, negative, neutral), the number of comments, and the number of likes.
P86: The mBERT works on cross-lingual texts of reviews written in 104 languages. It achieved 89-93% sentiment accuracy in reviews written in English/Standard Chinese and 82-86% in reviews written in Cantonese. mBERT's low-cost deployment and adaptability features efficiently perform automated text mining reviews.
P88: RoBERTa works on cross-lingual texts of reviews as well and attained 92-95% sentiment accuracy in reviews written in English/Standard Chinese and 86-90% in reviews written in Cantonese. It achieved a 3-5% increase in topic F1 score in reviews written in all languages compared to mBERT.
P90: DistilmBERT classifies reviews using the mBERT model and attained 87-91% sentiment analysis accuracy in reviews written in English/Standard Chinese and 80-84% in reviews written in Cantonese. It is 60% faster and performs analysis with 40% fewer parameters, which gives it the capacity to perform real-time analysis of reviews, especially in scenarios with low-computation deployment.
P94: 3. A user-friendly visual interface will be used to present to the users the results of their analyses using charts and structured data. The visual interface will allow users to easily see what the key feedback from their reviews was, helping them to make informed decisions based on their data.

P113: Literature Review
P115: The rise of e-commerce as a shopping channel began around 2010 with the widespread adoption of various electronic payment methods. It then rapidly expanded across various sectors (from books and electronics to clothing, home appliances, and almost all categories, resulting in a significant increase in online retail sales). By around 2020, with the popularity of smartphones, e-commerce developed into a dominant trend. The emergence of artificial intelligence brought even more possibilities to e-commerce, prompting us to explore whether it can be integrated with AI models for better development. In this context, Shang Yuli (2024) proposed a rather clear overall framework that combines the collection of reviews from e-commerce platforms with model analysis and classification, particularly highlighting the value of topic modeling after sentiment analysis. "However, we are not merely imitating the overall structure; instead, we have introduced certain innovations. First, the web scraping tool used in this case relies on a third-party desktop application that requires users to download and install it. We aimed to find a web scraping tool that can be implemented directly in Python code, making the scraping process more flexible and integrated, achieving an end-to-end solution. Additionally, we added a final step: allowing the LLM model to assist users in automatically generating a report and a chart. This means that after loading the sentiment analysis, the large language model will visualize the data. As a result, regardless of the user's role, they can obtain richer and more valuable information through our final report and chart."
P118: "Following this framework, as I just mentioned, we have adopted a similar structure. Therefore, we will also look at the processes of other similar cases and the tools they used. By referencing these, we can decide whether to improve based on the solutions in the cases or find excellent examples to learn from. I found that the earliest use of Scrapy for static web scraping has its advantages in simplicity but is almost limited to static web pages. It struggles with modern e-commerce platforms like Taobao that use JavaScript for dynamic rendering, which can easily trigger anti-scraping mechanisms, leading to data acquisition failures. To address this issue, Lu Chengyi et al. (2023) researched using headless browsers (like Selenium) to simulate user actions for scraping e-commerce reviews, which is already considered an excellent scraping method. However, through my research, I discovered a new domestic scraping tool called DrissionPage. This tool is powerful, simple, and flexible, which is exactly what we need. Yet, due to its novelty, it is challenging to find existing cases that use it. Therefore, we will manually write the scraping code, and DrissionPage has passed testing with outstanding performance. Thus, we have confirmed the use of this part of the technology stack for scraping."
P121: As for the preprocessing of crawled data, since we will also need to conduct sentiment analysis later, clear word segmentation is an essential foundation, laying the groundwork for the subsequent analysis. This structure, where we preprocess first and then perform sentiment analysis, was also researched through some case studies that included both processes. Yang Lihua (2023) first used jieba word segmentation for data preprocessing, then compared SnowNLP with BosonNLP for sentiment analysis, finding that SnowNLP had the advantage of clearer scoring, with a simple process and short time consumption. However, the downside was the lack of distinction between positive and negative emotions, which made it less intuitive. One of the advantages of BosonNLP's sentiment analysis method is its larger score range and ability to differentiate between positive and negative emotions, but it takes longer. Although BosonNLP provides more intuitive results, SnowNLP's scoring accuracy is superior to BosonNLP's when considering accuracy. However, Lu Chengyi et al. (2023) also preprocessed the data first through Jieba word segmentation, then expanded the dictionary of the Word2Vec word vector model using the dictionary expansion method. They extended the traditional sentiment lexicons (such as CNKI HowNet, Taiwan University NTUSD, BosonNLP Sentiment Lexicon). We found that, regardless of the rich tools and methods they later used, both Lu Chengyi et al. (2023) and Yang Lihua (2023) used Jieba word segmentation. Therefore, for us, this is a very useful word segmentation tool to reference. In the sentiment analysis phase, we observed that no tools other than Chinese sentiment analysis were mentioned in the cases we researched. This is because I noticed a certain angle missing, as almost no one is analyzing users from the Hong Kong, Macau, and Taiwan regions, where the predominant language is still Chinese. Due to the need to align with mainland e-commerce platforms, simplified Chinese may be used, or traditional Chinese may be retained, but considering that users in these regions still largely use Chinese, we focused on tools that perform excellently in Chinese.

P160: 3.5 Architecture or High-level System Design
P161: Data Collection
P162: Technology: DrissionPage, Python
P163: Process: Using DrissionPage in Python as core technology, it will simulate human operation. This mechanism allows the application to bypass the anti-scraping from Taobao, Jingdong through simulating the login operation. In the process, a browser instance is launched with ChormiumPage() and a designated product page will be entered by using get(‘designated URL’). Then use wait.ele_loaded() to wait for page resources loaded. Once the elements are loaded completely, functions like eles() could be used to locate the reviews. Data like user ids, text reviews, ratings, and dates will be extracted and stored in a raw review database.
P165: Data Preprocessing
P166: Technology: Regular Expression, Jieba, LLM (e.g., GPT, Claude, Grok)
P167: Process: In this process, noise such as irrelevant punctuation, special symbols, HTML tags, and extra whitespace is removed using Regex. Then, Jieba is utilized for precise Chinese word segmentation, while NLTK handles tokenization and stemming for English text. To ensure privacy protection, sensitive private information such as personal names, addresses, and phone numbers is identified and filtered out using the LLM's named entity recognition capabilities, as the raw dataset could unintentionally leak such details. The cleaned and tokenized results are then stored in a processed review database.
P170: Data Storage
P171: Technology: MySQL
P172: Process: Three databases will be involved in the system for data storage which are raw review, processed review, and analysis results.
P174: Sentiment Analysis
P175: Technology: PyTorch, Pre-trained Models (e.g., RoBERTa, DistilmBERT, mBERT)
P176: Process: In this process, pre-trained models such as RoBERTa, DistilBERT, and mBERT are invoked. These models are fine-tuned using relevant public datasets related to product commentary (e.g., consumer electronics reviews). This fine-tuning process adapts the general language understanding of the pre-trained models to the specific domain and task of our system, improving accuracy in identifying subtle sentiments. The outputs of the trained model include category review (such as battery, imaging), sentiment tendency (Positive, negative, neutral), and sentiment score. The analyzed results will be stored for further aggregation and visualization.
P178: Text Summarization
P179: Technology: LLM, Python
P180: Process: This module calls an external LLM API, feeding it the aggregated and analyzed results from the database. These LLM-generated summaries and recommendations constitute the Result Summary function, providing insightful conclusions from the large volume of reviews.
P182: User Interface
P183: Technology: Web dashboard (HTML, JS, etc.)
P184: Process: In order to present the result to users, an interactive web dashboard is built. It features with several functions: an input box for users to submit a designated product page URL; a selection box for users to specify their role whether the user is buyer or seller; an analysis button to trigger sentiment analysis and text summarization processes. The dashboard then dynamically displays the result using various graphic charts and presents text-based summarization and suggestion.
